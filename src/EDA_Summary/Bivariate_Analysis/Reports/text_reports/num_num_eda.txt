### 5. Anomalies & Limitations Noted in the Data

1. **Zero‑variance pairs** – Mutual information is exactly 0 for several pairs (e.g., Age ↔ Unit_Price, Age ↔ Total_Amount, Unit_Price ↔ Discount). This signals that the variables are statistically independent; any observed association is due to random noise, not a true relationship.

2. **Extreme p‑values** – The Pearson p‑value for Unit_Price ↔ Total_Amount is reported as “0,” indicating a p‑value smaller than the machine’s precision limit. This underscores the statistical certainty of the linear link.

3. **Inconsistent directionality in MI** – For most pairs the two MI columns (feature 1 → feature 2 and feature 2 → feature 1) are identical, confirming symmetric dependence (e.g., Quantity ↔ Total_Amount). However, a few pairs (Age ↔ Quantity, Quantity ↔ Delivery_Days) show asymmetry, suggesting that the direction of information flow differs slightly—potentially reflecting that one variable has more distinct values or a more deterministic influence on the other.

4. **Sample size uniformity** – All analyses use the same 15 000‑record sample, so the p‑values are comparable across metrics; however, the sheer size may inflate the sensitivity to tiny correlations (e.g., Age ↔ Quantity Pearson r = 0.011), which are statistically significant but practically irrelevant.

### 6. Recommendations for Stakeholders

| Recommendation | Rationale |
|----------------|-----------|
| **Prioritize pricing analytics** – Invest in models that forecast revenue based on unit price changes, as this variable exhibits the strongest linear (Pearson r = 0.835) and rank (Spearman ρ = 0.868) relationship with total amount. |
| **Bundle & volume incentives** – Since Quantity ↔ Total_Amount shows a moderate positive correlation (r = 0.256, MI = 0.36), targeted bundles or volume discounts can lift total sales without heavily compromising price. |
| **Refine discount strategy** – The negative monotonic link (Spearman ρ = –0.0835) suggests that broad, indiscriminate discounts may reduce total revenue; consider tiered or performance‑based discounting. |
| **Monitor delivery metrics separately** – Delivery days have weak, non‑significant relationships with other variables; focusing on delivery speed may improve customer satisfaction but is unlikely to impact revenue directly. |
| **Leverage age and rating for segmentation, not revenue prediction** – Their negligible correlations indicate they are poor predictors of purchase size or price; they can be used for customer‑experience initiatives (e.g., age‑specific promotions, rating‑driven loyalty tiers) rather than financial forecasting. |
| **Validate with higher‑frequency data** – The current 15 000‑record snapshot may mask short‑term fluctuations; periodic re‑analysis (e.g., monthly) will confirm whether the strong price‑revenue link persists. |

**Conclusion** – The bivariate analysis unequivocally identifies **unit price** and **order quantity** as the primary drivers of **total sales amount**, with a robust linear and monotonic relationship confirmed across Pearson, Spearman, Kendall, and mutual information metrics. All other examined variables (age, rating, discount, delivery days, etc.) exhibit either negligible or very weak associations, highlighting that revenue‑focused decisions should concentrate on pricing and volume levers rather than on customer age or rating characteristics.
