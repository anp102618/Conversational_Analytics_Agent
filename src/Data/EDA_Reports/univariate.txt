**Executive Summary – Categorical Distribution Overview**

---

### 1. Dominant categories & top‑performing segments  

| Feature | Dominant Category (Count / % ) | Top‑5 Segments (by count) |

*Key take‑aways*  

* **Gender** is almost balanced, with a slight female majority.  
* **City** distribution is relatively even; the top 10 cities each account for ~8–9 % of records, indicating a fairly uniform geographic spread.  
* **Product_Category** is led by **Electronics**, followed by **Fashion** and **Home & Kitchen**, together representing >65 % of the product mix.  
* **Product** items are numerous (30 distinct values); the five most frequent products (Smartwatch, Tablet, Headphones, Smartphone, Laptop) together comprise ~21 % of all product records.  
* **Payment_Method** shows a clear preference for **Credit Card**, which alone accounts for nearly one‑fifth of transactions.  
* **Returned** status is heavily skewed toward **No** (87.8 % of cases), implying returns are a minority event.

---

### 2. Cardinality, uniqueness & entropy insights  

| Feature | Unique Count | Cardinality % | Normalized Entropy | Interpretation |
|---------|--------------|--------------|--------------------|----------------|
| **Gender** | 3 | 0.02 % | 0.73 | Low diversity – only three categories, nearly balanced. |
| **City** | 12 | 0.08 % | 0.9996 | Moderate diversity; 12 distinct cities provide a fairly rich geographic signal. |
| **Product_Category** | 6 | 0.04 % | 0.9679 | Very low cardinality; categories are broad and mutually exclusive. |
| **Product** | 30 | 0.20 % | 0.9827 | High cardinality; 30 distinct product names indicate fine‑grained product variety. |
| **Payment_Method** | 6 | 0.04 % | 0.9994 | Low cardinality; payment types are limited to six common methods. |
| **Returned** | 2 | 0.013 % | 0.5353 | Binary flag with strong class imbalance (94 % “No”). |

*Observations*  

* **Product** exhibits the highest cardinality and near‑maximum entropy, reflecting a wide, diverse set of individual items.  
* **City** and **Payment_Method** have modest cardinalities with high normalized entropy, meaning the categories are sufficiently distinct to be informative without being overly fragmented.  
* **Gender** and **Returned** are low‑entropy, low‑cardinality features, suitable for simple encoding (e.g., label encoding).  

---

### 3. Rare categories / long‑tail distributions  

* **Product** – The tail is long: the top 10 products account for ~21 % of all product records, while the remaining 25 product values each represent <5 % of the total, with several (e.g., *Data Science Book*, *Novel*, *Yoga Mat*) below 3 % each.  
* **City** – The top 10 cities each represent ~8–9 % of records; the remaining 2 cities (not listed in the top‑10) together make up <2 % of the data, indicating a long‑tail of smaller urban markets.  
* **Payment_Method** – The six methods are evenly spread (15–19 % each); no pronounced long‑tail, but **COD** (15.86 %) is the least common method.  
* **Returned** – The “Yes” class (12.21 %) is a clear minority; within this class, individual return reasons (if any) are not captured, but the class itself is rare relative to the overall dataset.  

*Implication*: Features with many low‑frequency categories (especially **Product**) may benefit from **grouping** (e.g., clustering similar products, creating “Other” buckets) or **frequency‑based encoding** to avoid sparsity in downstream models.

---

### 4. Data quality concerns  

* **Missing values** – All six categorical features have **0 missing counts**; the dataset is fully populated for these variables.  
* **Sparse coverage** – No missingness, but **Product** (30 unique values) and **City** (12 unique values) have relatively low **unique_percentage** (0.20 % and 0.08 % respectively), meaning each category is well‑represented.  
* **Class imbalance** – The **Returned** target variable is heavily imbalanced (87.8 % “No”), which could affect model performance if not addressed (e.g., via resampling or class‑weighting).  

Overall, data quality appears high regarding completeness; the primary concern is the **imbalance** in the target and the **high cardinality** of the **Product** feature.

### 1. Central Tendency & Dispersion

| **Unit_Price** | 7,418 | 2,481 | 13,958 | **1.88** |

*Key observations*

* **Unit_Price** and **Total_Amount** have *much* higher standard deviations than their means, reflecting strong right‑skewness and heavy tails.

### 2. Distribution Shape, Skewness & Normality  

| Variable | Skewness | Kurtosis | Mean‑Median Gap | Normality (p‑value) |
|----------|----------|----------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Age | **+0.86** (moderately right‑skewed) | **3.88** (leptokurtic) | +0.30 | 0 (reject) |
| Quantity | **+1.12** (right‑skewed) | **0.48** (platykurtic) | –0.10 | 0 |
| Unit_Price | **+3.58** (strongly right‑skewed) | **23.38** (very leptokurtic) | +4,937 | 0 |
| Discount | **+0.32** (slightly right‑skewed) | **‑0.48** (platykurtic) | +2.0 | 8 × 10⁻¹¹¹ |
| Rating | **‑0.33** (slightly left‑skewed) | **‑0.33** (platykurtic) | –0.03 | 4 × 10⁻⁸⁰ |
| Delivery_Days | **+0.21** (slightly right‑skewed) | **‑0.26** (platykurtic) | +0.48 | 3 × 10⁻³⁷ |
| Total_Amount | **+8.68** (extremely right‑skewed) | **218.9** (extremely leptokurtic) | +9,339 | 0 |

*Interpretation*  

* All variables **fail** formal normality tests (p = 0).  
* **Skewness** is positive for the majority of variables, especially **Unit_Price** and **Total_Amount**, indicating long right‑hand tails.  
* **Kurtosis** is markedly high for **Unit_Price**, **Total_Amount**, and **Age**, implying that the data contain heavy tails and peaked peaks relative to a normal distribution.  
* **Rating** and **Discount** are the only variables with negative skew, but the magnitude is modest.

### 5. Actionable Recommendations  

| Recommendation | Rationale | Suggested Technique |
|----------------|-----------|---------------------|
| **Investigate zero discount entries** (1,740 zeros) | May represent data entry errors, free‑item promotions, or placeholder values that distort summary statistics. | Flag as “questionable”; consider imputing with the variable’s median (10) or removing if deemed erroneous. |
| **Apply log‑transformation** to **Unit_Price** and **Total_Amount** | Both variables exhibit extreme skewness (skew > 3) and very high kurtosis, inflating mean and variance. | `log1p(x)` or `log(x)` prior to analysis; re‑evaluate descriptive stats and outlier bounds. |
| **Winsorize or cap extreme values** in **Unit_Price** (max ≈ 309 k) and **Total_Amount** (max ≈ 1.2 M) | Outliers represent a small % of rows but dominate dispersion metrics, biasing models. | Set upper caps at, e.g., the 99th percentile (≈ 65 k for Unit_Price, ≈ 150 k for Total_Amount) or use robust scaling (e.g., robust Z‑score). |
| **Review low‑cardinality variables** (Quantity, Discount) | Their limited unique values suggest they may be better treated as categorical or binned variables. | Encode as factors or create bins (e.g., quantity = {0‑1, 2‑3, >3}) to stabilize variance. |
| **Standardize/normalize** after transformation | To bring all variables onto comparable scales for modeling (e.g., regression, clustering). | Use robust scaling (median & IQR) after log‑transform to mitigate remaining skew. |
| **Document data‑quality notes** for stakeholders | Zero‑discount proportion (11.6 %) and high cardinality of price/amount need explanation (e.g., promotional pricing, separate price tiers). | Add metadata notes; consider creating a “discount_flag” variable to capture zero‑discount cases explicitly. |
| **Re‑evaluate modeling assumptions** | Normality tests are all significant; parametric models may be inappropriate. | Prefer tree‑based models, robust regression, or non‑parametric methods; if parametric, use bootstrapped confidence intervals. |

---

**Bottom Line**  
The dataset is complete but heavily skewed, with pronounced outliers in monetary variables, a non‑trivial proportion of zero discounts, and a mix of low‑ and high‑cardinality features. Transforming the highly skewed monetary fields (log‑scale) and applying outlier capping/winsorization will reduce distortion, improve model performance, and yield more reliable insights. Zero‑discount values should be examined and either cleaned or explicitly flagged. All other variables are relatively clean, with acceptable missing‑value and negative‑value records.
